Kayaan Freight GTM3 / 12
Speaker notes

The largest market (enterprise 3PLs) is the wrong beachhead. HappyRobot raised a reported $150M Series C in Aug 2026 and lists DHL, Ryder, Schneider and Werner. Augment and Vooma sell to large brokerages. A seed-stage company with "SOC 2 in progress" will lose that security review.

New authorities look easy because there are many of them, but they have low load volume, they churn, and FMCSA's Motus rollout has disrupted new registrations. The sweet spot is the established small brokerage. It has enough loads for per-load revenue to matter, it is now exposed after Montgomery, and it has no in-house compliance team. Insurance commentary (RPS via The Insurer, Sept 2026) suggests tighter underwriting favors larger brokers with established vetting. Kayaan can give a small broker that kind of process for a per-load price.

The gap

The market split into entity vetting and enterprise desk agents. Nobody owns per-load proof of care for the small broker.

Why a broker adds Kayaan instead of switching

Don't ask them to rip anything out. Keep Highway or Carrier411 for onboarding. Kayaan covers what happens after the rate con, which is where 2026 fraud moved (Highway's Q3 report ties compromised communications to about 40% of stolen loads). At the end of every load it seals the record a plaintiff's attorney or underwriter will ask for.

The three reasons it converts

  • Liability: "reasonable care" is now the question in court. Shield's audit trail is the answer, load by load.
  • Labor: check calls and POD chasing are the hours a 15-person desk can't spare. A desk rep handles 3 to 4 loads a day (company claim).
  • Driver friction: owner-operators publicly complain about heavy ID flows (Overdrive, 2026). Kayaan is built by a fleet that lives on the driver side.

Three plays, one priority

Lead with signal-based outbound around a free carrier readout. Run the insurance channel in parallel. Build the driver loop third.

Lead · Days 1 to 90

1 · The Reasonable Care Readout

Offer: "Send the MC numbers of 10 carriers you booked last week. Within 24 hours you get a one-page readout: conditional or unsatisfactory safety ratings, authority age, insurance on file, recent MCS-150 contact changes, and what your file would show a plaintiff today."

Then: "Want this sealed automatically on every load? Run Shield on your next 20."

Why it works: it gives value before the demo, it uses public FMCSA data only, and it is the exact failure in Montgomery (a carrier with a conditional rating).

Buyer: owner or VP Ops · Cost: about $900/mo tools (est.)

Parallel · Days 15 to 90

2 · Renewal Desk

Channel: transportation insurance agents and wholesalers. Several (e.g., Higginbotham) have made broker liability a focus since the ruling.

Offer to agents: a free Carrier Selection Readiness review for their broker clients before renewal. Shield's 20-load file becomes underwriting evidence.

Why it works: agents need insurable brokers, brokers trust their agent, and the intro arrives warm with a deadline attached.

Buyer: same, introduced by agent · Cost: founder time + co-branded one-pager

Build · Days 60+

3 · Verified Once

Loop: every driver Shield verifies (consented) can reuse that identity on the next Kayaan-protected load from any broker. Carriers on the KAY app become pre-verified, and when a carrier hauls for a non-Kayaan broker, the driver can send "I'm Kayaan Verified" with a link.

Why it works: it connects Kayaan's two sites into one network. Verification friction falls for every broker added.

Needs product + legal (consent, data retention) · measure as a distribution channel

Not recommended now: paid ads, generic content, or SEO-first. The buyer pool is 25k named entities, so account-level precision beats reach. AI voice cold calling is also out: TCPA risk, and the wrong first impression for a fraud-prevention brand.

The first 30 days

One controlled experiment answers the only question that matters early: does the readout create pilots that convert?

Design

  • 300 accounts scored ≥ 60, split randomly into two arms of 150
  • Arm A: Reasonable Care Readout offer → 20-load pilot
  • Arm B (control): current offer, "Run Shield on your next 20 loads," same targeting and same sender
  • Parallel, not randomized: 3 insurance agents briefed, intros tracked separately
  • Pilot intake form replaces the open Calendly: TMS, loads/wk, lanes, renewal month

Budget

About $1,500 one-time (domains, inbox warm-up, enrichment credits, readout template) + about $900/mo tools. Founder time: about 6 demo hours plus 2 hours of agent calls. (est.)
