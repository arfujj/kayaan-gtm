Kayaan Freight: Lane Radar and the post-Montgomery GTM plan

Prepared by Arfaan Jeddy · November 20, 2026 · Based only on public information. Nothing here has been validated by Kayaan. Hypothetical figures are labeled ASSUMPTION and mock records are labeled MOCK.

Where the opportunity is: Kayaan's site already leads with Montgomery v. Caribe Transport II (SCOTUS, 9 to 0, May 14, 2026) and Shield's audit trail. The message is right. What's missing is a repeatable way to put it in front of the brokers who need it most, and the insurance agents and underwriters who now ask how brokers vet carriers. Driver ID checks are becoming common (Highway Know Your Driver, E3 via Transfix, Verified Carrier, Arthur), so the durable angle is the full per-load proof-of-care file, sold per load to small and mid brokers with no compliance team.

Recommended strategy: lead with Shield only, target established 10 to 75 person brokerages, and acquire them through Lane Radar (signal-based outbound built on public FMCSA data plus a free Reasonable Care Readout). Run an insurance-agent referral channel in parallel. Convert through a 20-load pilot that has signed success criteria and ends with a Reasonable Care File.

2 · Market, segments and buying triggers

2.1 Why now

  • Legal: Montgomery removed the FAAAA preemption defense for negligent selection. The live question in litigation is now whether the broker used reasonable care.
  • Insurance: RPS describes a "capacity issue" for broker contingent liability. Underwriters are expected to examine vetting processes, and poorly performing accounts can be priced up to 5x higher (The Insurer, Sept 2026). Commentators note the tightening may favor larger brokers with established procedures.
  • Fraud moved downstream: Highway's 2026 reports show record Q1 fraud, a shift from fake identities to abusing existing trust, and compromised communications linked to about 40% of stolen loads in Q3. Checking the entity at onboarding is no longer enough. What happens after the rate con is where fraud now lands.
  • Regulatory friction: FMCSA's Motus registration (May 2026) adds ID checks for new registrants, but the federal broker penalty fix is still pending. Enforcement will not solve this for brokers in 2027.

6.6 Automation vs human boundaries

  • Automated: data pulls, enrichment, scoring, brief and draft generation, reply classification, MC parsing, readout data assembly, CRM stage updates, dashboards.
  • Human, always: approving every first-touch email (month 1 to 2; then sampled once edit rate is below 10%), readout QA before sending, every call, demo, pilot scoping and pricing, anything sent to an insurance agent.
  • Never: scraping LinkedIn or gated sites, automated cold SMS or AI voice calls to prospects (TCPA), sending without an opt-out, implying legal protection.

10 · The nine questions, answered

QuestionAnswer
Why this customer?Established 10 to 75 person brokers have Montgomery exposure, real load volume, no compliance staff, fast decisions, and less attention from enterprise agent vendors.
Why this problem?Proving reasonable care per load is now a legal and insurance need, and fraud has moved to the post-booking window that onboarding tools don't cover.
Why Kayaan?Shield already produces the per-load trail. Operator founders add credibility, the driver-side app makes verification easier, and per-load pricing fits small P&Ls.
Why now?The ruling came in May 2026, insurers are tightening in Sept 2026, and fraud hit record levels in 2026. Brokers' next renewals arrive within the year.
Why this channel?A finite, public list of 25k accounts favors precision outbound. The readout gives value first, and agents bring warm, deadline-driven intros.
Why will it convert?The demo runs on the prospect's own carriers, the pilot has signed criteria, and the pilot ends with an artifact (the Reasonable Care File) the buyer needs for renewal.
What will it cost?About $4.5k to $5k in tools and services over 90 days plus one operator, with base-case CAC around $7.7k (assumption).
What can we validate in 30 days?Reply and readout rates, meetings, pilots started with criteria, Arm A vs control, domain health, and agent willingness. Pilot → paid is read by day 45 to 60.
Why trust me?I've built and run this kind of engine end to end (13k+ leads enriched, about 30k emails/mo, 10 clients and $18K to $23K MRR at my agency), and I built a working prototype of this one on Kayaan's real data source before being asked.
